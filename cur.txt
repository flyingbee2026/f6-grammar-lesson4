TABLE 3x2
  R0: ['Pattern 1 — Calling for Immediate Action \\n Target Formula: There is an urgent need for [doer] to [base verb phrase].', 'Pattern 1 — Calling for Immediate Action \\n Target Formula: There is an urgent need for [doer] to [base verb phrase].']
  R1: ['Basic:', 'The government can build more sports facilities.']
  R2: ['Upgraded:', 'There is an urgent need for the government to invest in free, accessible sports facilities in every district.']
P: Teaching note: “There is an urgent need for” is followed by [doer] + TO + base verb — never the base verb on its own (“for the government invest”) or a gerund (“for the government investing”). Use it when the problem cannot wait; “pressing” and “growing” can replace “urgent”.

TABLE 3x2
  R0: ['Pattern 2 — Purpose-First Recommendation \\n Target Formula: To [goal], [doer] should make it a priority to [base verb phrase].', 'Pattern 2 — Purpose-First Recommendation \\n Target Formula: To [goal], [doer] should make it a priority to [base verb phrase].']
  R1: ['Basic:', 'Schools can organise more sports activities and sports days.']
  R2: ['Upgraded:', 'To boost students’ physical fitness, schools should make it a priority to add a daily exercise break to the timetable.']
P: Teaching note: “make it a priority to” takes the BASE verb (“to add”, never “to adding”). Opening with “To + goal” replaces the vague “so that” and shows the marker your purpose in the first three words.

TABLE 3x2
  R0: ['Pattern 3 — Shared Responsibility \\n Target Formula: [Doer], in collaboration with [second party], should [verb phrase].', 'Pattern 3 — Shared Responsibility \\n Target Formula: [Doer], in collaboration with [second party], should [verb phrase].']
  R1: ['Basic:', 'Parents can exercise with their children at weekends.']
  R2: ['Upgraded:', 'Parents, in collaboration with schools, should encourage their children to adopt a more active lifestyle.']
P: Teaching note: “in collaboration with” = working together with. It is far stronger than “and” or “also” and shows the marker that you understand shared responsibility. The doer is swappable: “The government, in collaboration with district councils, should …”.
P: Teacher’s note on the design: the three patterns are deliberately kept to ONE clause. Endings of the “, so as to …” / “, thereby + –ing” type were left out this time — level 3 students lose control of the main clause as soon as they open a second one. Add the endings back only when the class can hold the short form.

Write a letter to the editor of the Hong Kong Young Post, suggesting ONE way in which our city can improve the physical well-being of its young people.']
P: Task requirements:
P: Make ONE suggestion (100 words) about improving physical well-being, and open your paragraph with ONE of today’s three patterns as the topic sentence.
P: 
P: Follow the chain: [Topic sentence] → [Explain] → [Example / evidence] → [Round off].
P: 
P: Teacher’s three suggested paragraphs — any ONE of them answers the task
P: Suggested paragraph 1 — the government (topic sentence uses Pattern 1)
P: There is an urgent need for the government to invest in free, accessible sports facilities in every district.  [Topic sentence — Pattern 1]Young people are not lazy; the problem is that the nearest pitch is often booked out months in advance, and a private gym costs more than most families can spare.  [Explain]Take a Form 5 student in Kwun Tong: with a floodlit running track and a basketball court five minutes from home, an hour of exercise becomes the easiest part of her evening instead of a two-bus journey.  [Example]Facilities within walking distance turn good intentions into daily habits, and that is the cheapest health policy a city can buy.  [Round off]
P: Suggested paragraph 2 — schools (topic sentence uses Pattern 2)
P: To boost students’ physical fitness, schools should make it a priority to add a daily exercise break to the timetable.  [Topic sentence — Pattern 2]Two PE lessons a week cannot undo six hours of sitting still, and students who never move at school rarely choose to move afterwards, however warmly their teachers praise a healthy lifestyle.  [Explain]Fifteen minutes of skipping, badminton or jogging before the first lesson would give every student — not only the team players — a daily dose of activity.  [Example]Such a break costs the timetable fifteen minutes and buys students a lifetime of healthier habits.  [Round off]
P: Suggested paragraph 3 — parents (topic sentence uses Pattern 3)
P: Parents, in collaboration with schools, should encourage their children to take up a sport and stick with it.  [Topic sentence — Pattern 3]Children copy what they see at home, and enthusiasm for a new sport fades quickly when nobody joins in, drives them to practice or turns up to watch.  [Explain]A weekend hike, a family badminton session in the park, or simply a parent cheering from the sidelines turns a hobby into a habit that no textbook can teach.  [Example]Active families raise active young people, and that habit outlasts any school programme or government campaign.  [Round off]
P: Teacher’s note: each paragraph makes ONE suggestion; the topic sentence is the only place where a pattern is required. Check the chain [Topic sentence] → [Explain] → [Example / evidence] → [Round off].
